# ADAM revision self-audit

This is a targeted self-review of the reopened findings, not an independent audit or a deployment approval. The existing token, staking architecture, immutable split, constructor interfaces, pool configuration and dependency versions were preserved. No transactions were broadcast. The independent reviewer should recheck the final code and deployment arguments before release.

| Severity | Reproduced finding | Resolution |
| --- | --- | --- |
| High | A 1 wei stake plus dust notification took all rewards parked before any stake | Parked rewards now stream over seven days only above a fixed 10,000,000 ADAM aggregate stake threshold. Accumulators are settled before stake changes; falling below the threshold pauses and restarts the remaining stream, without retroactive vesting. Tests cover dust attacks, threshold entry/exit, new holders, both tokens and conservation. |
| Medium | The process floor followed a price set by an attacker in the same transaction | Seed per-leg price checkpoints at construction. Quote against the greater of checkpoint and spot, and update the checkpoint only after successful buys. Failed buys cannot reset it. The supplied profitable 50 ETH-depth sandwich is refused, as is the 50 ETH pump in the real IMD fork test. |
| Medium | ETH-specified partial fills retained the fee on the whole request | Check the raw ETH pool delta against the expected complete fill in afterSwap; mismatches revert atomically. Empty swaps revert too. Tests check trader/Treasury balances after rejection. |
| Low | Exact-output trades paid a lower effective fee | Gross up both exact-output paths by bps/(10000-bps). A launch exact-output buy of the same ADAM now costs the same 1 ETH and pays the same 0.2 ETH as exact-input. Fee fuzzing covers the decay period. |
| Low | exit reverted after a separate unstake | Skip the withdrawal when stake is zero and still claim; repeat exit is harmless. |
| Low | Dust and two isolated failures could cause rerouting | Keep sub-1-gwei amounts pending without a failure stamp. Require at least four failures over three days, with bounded gaps; success, a skipped dust attempt or a long gap resets the streak. Hourly failing attempts still reroute in either direction. |
| Low | Failed retries never shrank | Halve the per-leg attempt cap after failure, with a 1-gwei floor and reset on success. In a 20 ETH-depth local pool, the 1 ETH attempt fails and the 0.5 ETH retry buys rewards. |
| Low | Missing README and self-audit | Added both, including exact deployment parameters, operational steps, reward eligibility and deviations. |
| Informational | Hook/Treasury are absent from the explicit exclusion set | Confirmed and documented the report's suggested documentation resolution. The immutable contracts have no staking, approval-to-Distributor or arbitrary-execution path. Constructor wiring and exclusion behavior remain unchanged. |
| Informational | Initialization delay consumed the anti-snipe interval | Start at the first nonempty successful swap, keeping separate initialization state. Delaying the first trade seven days still charges 20%; empty/reverted trades leave the clock unset. Hook flags remain 0x20cc. |

The three supplied proofs failed against the starting tree and passed after the fixes. Seven additional baseline probes reproduced the advisory behaviors before implementation. Default Foundry unit/fuzz checks cover split accounting, claims, exclusions, authorization, reentrancy, fee schedules, caps, stream timing and retries. Fork checks use Ethereum block 26,126,549 and exercise real buys, sells, minting through PositionManager, process, claims, tax measurement and sandwich refusal. See `.imd-responses.json` for finding IDs and reproduction details. Compiler: 0.8.26; fuzzing: 256 cases per property in the default profile. The revision run passed 93 permanent default tests, all three supplied proofs and six mainnet-fork tests. `forge build` and `forge fmt --check` passed. Application runtimes remain below EIP-170 and contain no DELEGATECALL, CALLCODE or SELFDESTRUCT under the protected check's opcode scan. Slither and Mythril were not run.

The fork run also corrected three pre-existing test assumptions: LP minting leaves 389 token wei of rounding dust at this configuration; dividend division can leave one reward wei; and the PNKSTR hook sells its collected tax during afterSwap. The revised tax test selects the original router's Swap event, excluding the hook's nested sell. It measures 1000 bps of actual output tax (1001 bps against spot including price impact).

Residual assumptions and operational responsibilities:

- Staking remains opt-in. Wallet transfers do not track dividends. Newly notified rewards belong to instantaneous stake, so just-in-time staking and sole-staker concentration remain possible. A holder meeting the 1% threshold can receive the backlog by staying eligible through the stream. The stream does not reconstruct historical wallet ownership, and repeated threshold exits can delay it.
- The checkpoint is a circuit breaker, not an external oracle or TWAP. Deployment prices must be checked independently. Small repeated adverse moves can consume the tolerance over multiple calls. A genuine large adverse repricing can block a leg until recovery or the observed-failure reroute; there is no owner override. A pool absent at construction cannot obtain a later price checkpoint.
- The 50/50 split is initially earmarked in ETH; failed legs may eventually reroute. Bounded failure gaps require keepers to continue hourly attempts during outages. If both pools are unusable, ETH stays held; neither contract can conjure a working exit market. Dust waits for future fees.
- PNKSTR's unverified hook and both reward markets are external dependencies. A changed tax or reduced liquidity may refuse buys. The size cap, anchored output minimum and adaptive retries bound individual attempts; they do not guarantee execution or eliminate all MEV.
- Hook ownership permits one pool initialization, fee reductions, two-step transfer and renunciation. It grants no Treasury/Distributor withdrawal power. The deployer owns the LP NFT and may withdraw liquidity. Only the hooked PoolKey pays the requested fees. Hook/Treasury exclusions remain implicit through lack of staking capability as documented in README.
